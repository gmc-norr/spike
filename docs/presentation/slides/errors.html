Sequencing errors drawn from base quality

Each synthetic base is wrong with probability p = 10^(−Q/10), Q being the quality drawn for it. A wrong base becomes one of the three others with equal probability. Indel errors are off by default (--indel-error-rate).

The error model is the Phred definition itself: a base of quality Q is wrong with probability ten to the minus Q over ten. Counts behind the table: real Q11 8,648 of 91,530 aligned bases, Q25 518 of 163,031, Q37 369 of 3,306,944; spike Q11 4,277 of 52,754, Q25 265 of 89,594, Q37 367 of 1,819,605. One caveat: the aligner soft-clips the worst read ends, and those bases have no reference position, so they are missing from this count. An earlier inspection found that real reads with a crashed tail carry far more errors than their qualities say.
