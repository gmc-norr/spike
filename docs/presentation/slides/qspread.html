The previous slide showed only the mean. Here is the spread. Panels A and B: at each cycle, the standard deviation of base quality across reads is the same for real and spike's reads, about 3.5 at the start rising to 6 or 7 at the end. Panel C asks a different question: how much does quality vary from one read to the next? Real reads differ a lot. One in eighteen is a perfect read, Q37 at every base, and one in twelve has a mean below Q33. spike's reads are all alike, between Q33 and Q37, with almost no perfect reads and no poor ones. The cause is the chain's one-base memory: it cannot know that a read is a good or a bad read overall. A first-order chain fitted directly to the real reads gives the same narrow spread as spike, 0.55, close to the 0.46 of real qualities shuffled between reads. The crashed 3′ ends on the next slide are one form of this. The reads spike replaced had the same spread as the rest of the real reads (SD 2.03), so the gap is not caused by which reads were compared.
