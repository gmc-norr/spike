Base quality model: a first-order Markov chain

The quality of base i is drawn from the observed qualities in the most specific bin holding ≥ 30 observations:

  1. P( Q(i) | i, b(i), B(Q(i−1)) )
  2. P( Q(i) | i, B(Q(i−1)) )
  3. P( Q(i) | i, b(i) ), also used at cycle 1
  4. P( Q(i) | i ), any number of observations

i: cycle; b(i): base; B bins the previous quality: Q0–9, 10–19, 20–29, ≥ 30. Estimated per mate from the donor pool; a draw is a resampled observed value. Bins with ≥ 30 observations in the example run: level 1, 3,167 of 4,832; level 2, 900 of 1,208; level 3, 1,208 of 1,208.

Base qualities are not independent along a read: a low-quality base is far more often followed by another low one. Here 2.9 percent of real bases are Q11, but after a low-quality base the next is Q11 almost 20 percent of the time. spike models this with a first-order Markov chain whose transition probabilities are estimated per cycle, per base and per mate from the sample's own reads, with fallbacks to coarser bins where data are sparse. Figure 5 shows the one-step transitions measured on real reads and on spike's reads in the same windows: they agree closely.
